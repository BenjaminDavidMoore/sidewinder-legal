    Chapter 1

    What Sidewinder does, and what you need before you begin

    Sidewinder takes a photo of a whitetail buck and gives you a Boone & Crockett style score in under a minute. This guide gets you from nothing installed to your first scored buck.

    You point the camera at a buck, tap the shutter, type the distance if you know it, and tap Score this buck. The app sends the photo to an AI model of your choosing. The model measures the rack (beams, tines, spread and mass) and Sidewinder adds those measurements up the way an official scorer would. You get a gross score, a range of uncertainty, and a full breakdown you can read line by line.

    Read this once

    Every score is an estimate, not an official Boone & Crockett measurement. Official scores need a certified measurer and a 60-day drying period. Use Sidewinder to decide whether a buck is worth a closer look, not to enter one in a record book. Sidewinder is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Boone and Crockett Club.

    How the AI part works, in plain terms

    Sidewinder has no server and no account of its own. There is nothing to sign up for and nothing to sign in to. Instead it talks directly to one of three AI companies on your behalf: Anthropic (makers of Claude), OpenAI (makers of ChatGPT), or Google (makers of Gemini). To do that it needs an API key from one of them. Think of an API key as a long password that lets an app use your account with that company. You pay that company directly for what you use, and the amounts are small: typically a few cents per score, depending on which model you pick. The app shows you the cost before you score anything.

    You only need one of the three. All three work the same way in the app. This guide recommends Claude, because it is the app's default and the engine the app has been tested against the most.

    What you need

    • A phone with a working camera. This guide shows the iPhone; the Android app has the same screens.
    • About 15 minutes for setup. Most of that is creating an account with the AI company and adding a small amount of credit.
    • A payment card for the AI company. Anthropic and OpenAI want prepaid credit before a key will work. Google has a free tier that is often enough to try the app.
    • Optional but helpful: a laser rangefinder. If the distance readout is visible in the photo, Sidewinder reads it for you.

    The setup, in order

    1. Install the app (Chapter 2).
    2. Open it and read the two first-launch screens (Chapter 3).
    3. Create an account with one AI company and get an API key (Chapter 4).
    4. Paste the key into Sidewinder, which tests it for you (Chapter 5).
    5. Score your first buck (Chapter 6).
    Chapter 2

    Installing the app

    While Sidewinder is in testing, iPhone users install it through Apple's TestFlight app, which works like a small private App Store. Once it is released, it installs from the App Store or Google Play like any other app and you can skip to Chapter 3.

    iPhone, through TestFlight

    1. Install TestFlight from the App Store Open the App Store on your iPhone, search for TestFlight (the icon is a blue circle with a white propeller), and install it. It is free and made by Apple. Sign in with your Apple ID if it asks.
    2. Accept your invitation You will get the invite one of two ways. By email: look for a message from TestFlight inviting you to test Sidewinder Scoring and tap View in TestFlight on your iPhone. By link: the person who invited you may send a link that starts with testflight.apple.com/join/. Open it on your iPhone and it will jump straight into TestFlight.
    3. Tap Accept, then Install TestFlight lists the app as Sidewinder Scoring, with an Accept button. Tap it, then tap Install. The icon on your home screen is labelled Sidewinder.

    Android

    Android testers are sent a Google Play testing link by the person who invited them. Open it on your phone, accept, and install from Google Play as usual.

    Permissions the app asks for

    Nothing is asked until you reach the Score tab, after the two first-launch screens in Chapter 3. Then your phone asks for camera access. Tap Allow. Without it you can still score photos from your photo library, but not shoot new ones from inside the app. The first time you take a shot, Sidewinder also asks whether to save photos to your album. That one is your choice and is explained in Chapter 7. The microphone is never used.

    Keeping it up to date

    During testing, new builds arrive through TestFlight, not the App Store. When one is available, TestFlight shows an Update button next to the app, and by default it also sends you a notification. You do not need to reinstall or re-enter your API keys after an update. Your keys and your scoring history stay on the phone.

    Builds expire

    Apple retires every TestFlight build 90 days after it is uploaded. If the app suddenly refuses to open and TestFlight says the build has expired, install the newest one from TestFlight. If there is none, tell the person who invited you.

    Sending feedback

    Because this is a TestFlight app, taking a screenshot inside Sidewinder pops up a Share Beta Feedback option. That sends the screenshot and your note straight to the developer, which is the fastest way to report something that looks wrong. If a score seems off, the most useful thing you can send is the Breakdown page from Chapter 6, which shows exactly what the model measured.

    Chapter 5

    Putting the key into Sidewinder

    On first launch the key goes into the "Bring your own key" screen, which tests it before it is saved. Later on, keys live in Settings → Keys. Either way the key goes only to the company it belongs to and is kept in your phone's keychain.

    On first launch

    1. Pick the provider Under Provider, tap Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini to match the key you made. The key field's label changes to match: Anthropic API key, OpenAI API key or Google AI API key.
    2. Leave the model alone, for now The Model row is set to that company's recommended model, with the most a single score could cost underneath. You can change it here or later in Settings. The key is tested against whichever model is showing.
    3. Paste the key and tap Save key Tap the paste button at the end of the field, or long-press and choose Paste. The Save button lights up once the key is long enough to be real. Saving is a test: the app asks the company a one-word question on your key, which costs a fraction of a cent. Nothing is stored until the company answers.
    4. Read the verdict "That key works" means it is saved. The provider's button gets a ✓ and the row now shows the first and last few characters with the middle hidden, and a small KEYCHAIN tag. If the test fails, the message tells you which problem it is: a mistyped key, an account with no credit, a key that cannot reach that model, or no signal. Fix it and tap Save key again.
    5. Tap Start scoring The button lights up as soon as one key is saved. The engine with a key becomes the engine the app uses. If you add keys for two or more, a Default provider picker appears at the bottom so you can choose.

    Adding or changing a key later

    Go to Settings → Keys. Each of the three companies has its own block with a status tag, the key row, and the same two folded how-tos. Tap Add (or Edit), paste, and tap Save key. In Settings, saving does not test the key on its own, so tap Test afterward. It asks the company whether the key works on the model you have picked.

    • Copy puts the saved key on the clipboard for 60 seconds, then clears it automatically so it does not linger where another app could read it.
    • Delete appears when you tap Edit on a saved key. The app asks first, because the company showed you that key only once. Deleting it from the phone does not revoke it. Do that on the company's website.
    • To score with a different company, add its key here, then pick it under Settings → Scoring → Scoring engine.
    Chapter 6

    Scoring your first buck

    Take or pick a photo, add the distance if you know it, and tap one button. A mounted rack or a clear trail-camera photo from your library is a good first test.

    The three tabs

    Along the bottom of every screen: Score (the camera and the way in), History (every buck you have scored on this phone), and Settings. You can switch tabs mid-way through and nothing is lost. A half-typed distance is still there when you come back.

    The Score tab, piece by piece

    • The viewfinder fills the screen. A caption on it tells you how to frame the buck. Head on or from behind gives the best view of the spread. A broadside shot shows the beams and tines. Once you have one photo, the caption changes to suggest a different angle.
    • Zoom chips (.5×, 1×, 3×) sit at the top left of the viewfinder, beside an Optical zoom only tag. Only the lenses your phone actually has are shown, and the app never goes past optical zoom. Digital zoom makes a rack measure badly, so it is deliberately not offered.
    • Shutter in the middle takes a photo. Library on the left picks one from your camera roll.
    • The photo strip appears above the viewfinder once you have a photo. You can gather up to three photos of the same buck. Tap a thumbnail to look at it, tap the × to drop it, and tap Back to camera to shoot another. The first photo is the primary one. More than three angles stops adding evidence and starts adding cost.
    • Distance to deer is a single row with a number and its unit. Tap the unit to switch between yards and meters. It is optional. If you leave it blank the model estimates the range, and the result will say "est. range". A known distance makes the scale more reliable. If you shot the photo through a rangefinder and the readout is visible in the frame, Sidewinder reads the number for you and tags it Auto-detected. That reading happens on the phone. Typing over it replaces it with yours, and the tag changes to Manual.
    • Score this buck appears once you have a photo, and sends everything off. If no key is set for the chosen engine, this is where you will be told to add one in Settings.

    The wait

    The scoring screen shows your lead photo, a progress bar that moves through Uploading, Processing and Receiving, and a clock. On the default settings expect well under a minute. It takes longer with more photos, and noticeably longer with Look closer turned on (Chapter 7). Cancel stops the run. If the model returns something garbled the app quietly tries once more. If it fails outright you get the reason in plain words, with Back and Retry buttons.

    Reading the result

    • Gross · Estimate is the headline: the gross typical score in inches, written the scorer's way in eighths (154 6/8", not 154.75). Beside it is the tolerance, how far off the model thinks it could be. Plus or minus six inches on a 150-inch buck is a solid estimate. The small line at the top right names the engine, how long it took, and the distance used.
    • Confidence is the model's own sense of how well it could see the rack. Green (70% and up) is good, amber means take it with a grain of salt, red (under 40%) means the photo did not show enough.
    • Estimated age is a bracket in half years (4½–5½), the way a biologist writes it.
    • The class scale is a ruler from 100 to 180 inches with a marker where this buck falls: Grow (100–120), Shooter (120–140), Prize (140–160), Trophy (160–170) and Record (170 and up), with a one-line summary underneath.
    • Model notes is the model telling you, in a sentence or two, what it saw and which number it is least sure of.

    The breakdown

    Tap See the breakdown to see the working. It has gross, deductions and net at the top, every measurement in inches (spread, both beams, each tine, each circumference), the B&C typical formula spelled out with your numbers in it, a How B&C scoring works button that opens a short guide with a labelled antler diagram, and a Scale & distance block saying whether the range was yours or estimated, the model's confidence and why, and the tolerance. When a score seems wrong, this is the page to look at first, and the page to send as feedback.

    History

    Every score is kept on the phone: the photos you sent, the prompt that went with them, and what came back. Each row shows the date, the engine and the distance. Tap one to reopen it exactly as it was. Swipe a row to the left to delete it. The app asks you to confirm, and the photo and the saved response go with it.

    Chapter 8

    What the numbers mean

    A short primer on Boone & Crockett typical scoring, so the Breakdown page reads like a score sheet and not a spreadsheet. The app has a longer version, with a labelled antler diagram, under "How B&C scoring works".

    Boone & Crockett scores a typical whitetail by adding up a fixed set of measurements, then subtracting how far the two sides differ. Everything is measured to the nearest eighth of an inch and written that way: 153 0/8", never 153.0.

    MeasurementWritten asWhat it is
    Inside spreadDThe widest gap between the main beams, at a right angle to the skull. Credited only up to the length of the longer beam.
    Main beamFFrom the lowest outside edge of the burr, along the outer curve, to the tip. One per side.
    Normal points (tines)G1, G2, G3…Each point rising off the top of the beam, tip down to where it meets the beam. G1 is the brow tine, then outward.
    Circumferences (mass)H1 – H4Girth of the beam at four places per side: the narrowest spot between the burr and G1, then between each pair of tines.
    Abnormal points—Points growing anywhere other than the top of the beam, or off another point. Deducted for a typical score, added for a non-typical one.

    Putting it together

    • Gross = spread + both beams + every normal point + all eight circumferences.
    • Deductions = for each beam, tine and circumference, the difference between left and right, all added up, plus any abnormal points.
    • Net = Gross − Deductions.

    That is why net is always lower than gross: B&C rewards symmetry, and every eighth of an inch one side out-measures the other comes off. Hunters usually talk in gross. Record books use net: 160" for the Awards book, 170" for the All-time book.

    What counts as a point

    A projection is a point when it is at least one inch long and longer than it is wide at one inch of length. Beam tips count as points but are not measured as tines. A 10-point buck is written 5 × 5.

    About the tolerance

    The ± figure next to the score is the model's honest range. It is judged against the headline score, not against any single tine. Five inches off on a 140-inch buck is a good estimate. Five inches off on a brow tine would not be, but that error rarely survives into the total. Trust a score more when the confidence is high, the distance was ranged rather than estimated, and you gave it more than one angle.

    Chapter 9

    When something goes wrong

    The messages you are most likely to see, what they mean, and what to do. A failed key test also has a Show detail link with the company's exact words.

    "No Anthropic API key set — add one in Settings" (or OpenAI, or Google AI)

    The engine picked under Settings → Scoring has no key. Either add one on the Keys tab (Chapter 5), or switch Scoring engine to the one you did add a key for.

    "That API key was rejected"

    The key was pasted short or has been revoked. Go to Settings → Keys, tap Edit on that key, paste it again, save, and tap Test. If you no longer have it, make a new key on the company's website and delete the old one there.

    "Your account is out of API credit"

    The key is fine but there is no money behind it, so the photo was never scored. Log in to that company's website (console.anthropic.com, platform.openai.com, or aistudio.google.com) and add credit, then tap Retry. Remember that API billing is separate from a Claude.ai, ChatGPT Plus or Google One subscription.

    "That key is not permitted to make this request" or "cannot reach the model"

    The key is real but is shut out of the model the app is set to use. Restricted or project-scoped keys do this, and new accounts sometimes need billing set up before the larger models open up. Make a key with full access, or pick a smaller model under Settings → Scoring → Model.

    Every Claude Fable 5.1 request fails, but Opus 5 works

    Your Anthropic organization is on zero data retention, which Fable 5.1 does not support. Switch the Model back to Claude Opus 5, or ask Anthropic to move the organization to standard 30-day retention.

    "You are sending requests faster than this account allows"

    A rate limit. Wait a few seconds and tap Retry. Free-tier Gemini keys hit this soonest, and have a daily cap that resets the next day.

    "…is overloaded right now" or "hit a server error on their end"

    The company is having trouble. Nothing is wrong with your key or your photo. Wait a moment and tap Retry. If it keeps happening it is an outage, and switching Scoring engine to another company you have a key for will get you a score in the meantime.

    "The photo is too large"

    Lower Max image size under Settings → Scoring and tap Retry.

    "Rejected the request as invalid"

    If you have edited the measurement prompt, that is the first thing to suspect. Settings → Scoring → Measurement prompt → Edit → Reset.

    "The model garbled its answer — trying once more"

    Nothing to do. The model returned something the app could not read, and it retries automatically. If it fails twice you get a Retry button. Trying a different photo or a bigger model usually clears it.

    "Camera access is off for Sidewinder"

    Open your phone's Settings app → scroll to Sidewinder → turn on Camera. You can still score photos from your library without it.

    The "Bring your own key" screen keeps coming back

    It returns on every launch until at least one key is saved. If you skipped it, add a key there or under Settings → Keys and it will stop.

    The distance field did not fill in from my rangefinder photo

    Reading the readout is best-effort. It needs the number and its unit to be legible in the frame. Just type the distance in. A typed number always wins over a detected one.

    The score seems way off

    Open the Breakdown and look at Scale & distance first. A wrong distance throws every measurement in the same direction. Then check which tine measurements look wrong. Common causes: the far side of the rack hidden behind the near side, a brow tine confused with a G2, or a photo taken with digital zoom on another camera.

    Things that help: a second photo from a different angle, a ranged distance, Look closer at 4 or 6, and image size at the ceiling. Then send the Breakdown as feedback so the developer can see it.

    How much is this costing me?

    The Cost per score footing on Settings → Scoring shows a typical figure and a ceiling for one photo, and, expanded, the ceiling for three. For the real number, each company has a usage page on its website. Prepaid credit means you can never be charged more than you put in.

    TestFlight says the build has expired

    Open TestFlight and install the newest build. If there is none listed, ask the person who invited you for a new one. Your keys and history survive an update.

    I got a new phone

    Install Sidewinder again. There is no account, so nothing carries over on its own: History lives on the old phone, and so do your API keys. Paste the keys in again on the new one. If you did not keep a copy, make new keys on the company's website.

    Before you ask for help

    • Which engine, model and Look-closer setting you were using.
    • The exact error text, or a screenshot of the Breakdown page. Never send your API key.
    • The app version from the bottom of Settings → General.
